# Starting location and detected city

The normal planner asks for one precise starting point, chosen by search, a GPS request initiated by the user, or a confirmed map pin. It then shows a compact **Exploring: [city]** display. The app resolves the starting point's provider-verified city identity through the existing city lookup to obtain validated city coordinates and timezone. These city coordinates never replace the exact starting point used for routing, transport distance or a return-to-start journey.

**Change city** reveals the optional destination search. A confirmed manually chosen city remains fixed when the starting point changes. The existing outside-start review and explicit consent still apply. **Cancel city change** keeps the previous confirmed destination. **Explore my starting city instead** returns to detection mode. Searching a city alone is insufficient as an exact start; use an address, hotel, station, actual GPS coordinates or a pin. To place a pin before choosing a start, use **Choose an area for a map pin** to open a real city map first.

No GPS is requested on page load. A pending or failed city lookup preserves the starting point and blocks planning until city selection is valid. Failures offer explicit retry and manual selection. Expiring evidence, mismatched returned localities or missing timezone data never produce a fictional/default city. Request cancellation and sequence checks prevent old city responses from overwriting newer starts or a manual city choice. Replacing a start in the same verified city reuses unexpired city evidence. A user-selected currency is preserved even if a city result arrives later.

Only Plan my day, Compare three plans or Optimize route requests planning. Form/city edits invalidate pending previews; accepted trips stay preserved. The optional `destinationMode` local-storage field is saved only with accepted itineraries. Existing saved trips without this field retain manual destination mode. Restoring a trip does not make a city lookup automatically; the existing explicit refresh-locations action is retained.

No backend route contract, city-area filtering, provider limits, secrets or dependencies change. Existing city lookup calls are accounted for by the provider guard. Tests cover GPS success/denial, searched starts, city-only rejection, city resolution failure/retry, stale responses, concurrent currency edits, another-city consent, distinct start/city coordinates, both planners, acceptance/reload and desktop/mobile layout.
